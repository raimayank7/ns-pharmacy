Pharmacy bill format: what a chemist’s bill must show

A chemist’s bill answers to two sets of rules: the drug rules, which care about who bought which medicine from which batch, and GST, which cares about tax. One good bill satisfies both.

What the drug rules ask for

A retail chemist keeps a record of every sale of prescription medicines. Under Rule 65 of the Drugs Rules, 1945, for medicines supplied in their original packs, this record may be kept as a serially numbered cash or credit memo instead of a separate prescription register. That memo should show:

  • the serial number and date,
  • the patient’s name and address,
  • the prescriber’s name (and address or registration number),
  • the name and quantity of each medicine,
  • for Schedule H, H1 and similar medicines, the manufacturer, batch number and expiry date.

Your store’s name, address and drug licence numbers go at the top, and a registered pharmacist supervises the sale.

What GST asks for

If the store is registered under GST, the same bill is a tax invoice: your GSTIN, a number of up to 16 characters, date, HSN code, quantity, taxable value, GST rate and amount, and place of supply. On a sale of ₹50,000 or more to a buyer without GSTIN, the buyer’s name, address and state are also needed (Rule 46 of the CGST Rules). A store under composition gives a bill of supply without GST, with the words "composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies". A store that is not registered gives a cash or credit memo.

A sample bill

#MedicineMfr.BatchExp.QtyMRPDisc.Amount
1Paracetamol 650 mg tabABC PharmaPX251108/272 Strip32.0010%57.60
2Amoxicillin 500 mg cap [H]XYZ LabsAM093403/271 Strip105.0010%94.50

Total at MRP ₹169.00, discount ₹16.90, bill total ₹152.10, rounded to ₹152. At 5% GST the taxable value inside ₹152.10 is ₹144.86 and the GST ₹7.24 (₹3.62 CGST + ₹3.62 SGST). The Schedule H note prints at the bottom because line 2 is a Schedule H medicine. (Names and figures are only an example.)

Mistakes to avoid

  • No batch or expiry on the bill. If a batch is recalled, you cannot tell which patients got it.
  • Charging above MRP. MRP is the ceiling and already includes GST; you may sell below it.
  • GST added on top of MRP. The tax is inside the MRP, never added on top.
  • Unnumbered bills. Use one running series per financial year, as the maker does: PH/26-27/0001 onwards.
Drug rules are enforced by your State Drugs Control office and differ a little in practice from state to state. Check with them for anything specific to your licence.
